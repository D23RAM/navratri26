Understanding Navratri

The nine nights, the goddess, and the many ways Hindus celebrate them.

What Navratri means

Nava is nine and ratri is night, so Navratri is "nine nights". The number is part of the festival's structure: nine nights, nine forms of the goddess, and, in many regions, a tenth day of victory.

Durga and Shakti

Durga is a major form of the Devi, the great goddess. In the Shakta tradition the goddess is the supreme power, Shakti. In the Devi Mahatmya, a text in the Markandeya Purana, the gods' combined energies take form as the goddess who defeats the buffalo demon Mahishasura. Navratri is closely associated with that story in many regions.

Why dates and practices vary

Navratri follows the Hindu lunar calendar, so its date on the Gregorian calendar changes every year. Calendar systems also differ: whether a month starts at the new moon or the full moon, and how a tithi (lunar day) is counted from the local sunrise, can move a festival by a day between places and communities.

Customs differ too. Some households fast, some dance, some install images of the goddess, and some do all three. This site explains traditions; it does not prescribe practice.
